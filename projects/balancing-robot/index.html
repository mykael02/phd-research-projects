Model and experiment limits

A side view of a two-wheeled inverted pendulum with a 1.2 kg body, 0.18 m centre-of-mass height above the axle, and 0.045 m wheel radius. Both controllers receive exact state measurements and the same external force at the body centre of mass. The nominal controllers stay fixed when you change the torque limit.

Physics runs at 200 Hz with fourth-order Runge–Kutta integration. A fall is declared at 45°; ground impact is not simulated. The camera follows the axle, and ground markers show movement relative to the starting point. The experiment ends at 20 simulated seconds. Changing initial tilt or torque limit resets the run.

No wheel slip, sensor noise, motor electrical dynamics, yaw, real hardware calibration, or safety guarantees. These are initial controllers, not a claim that either method is generally better. Use the Python experiment runner for reproducible recovery-time measurements.
