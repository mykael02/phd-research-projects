Definitions and interpretation

Every point is a separate, deterministic 10-second trial. Initial position and all velocities are zero. A signed horizontal force acts at the body centre of mass from 2.0 to 2.2 seconds. Gains, body parameters, exact state feedback, and the 200 Hz control rate stay fixed. There is no sensor noise, delay, payload change, wheel slip, or hardware validation in this study.

  • Recovered: no fall, and at least the final second stays within 1° tilt, 0.03 m of the starting position, and 0.03 m/s speed.
  • Upright, not settled: finishes 10 seconds without reaching 45° tilt, but fails the recovery definition.
  • Fallen: reaches 45° absolute tilt at a control-step endpoint. Simulation stops; ground impact is not modelled.

Recovery time is measured from the end of the push; zero-force trials use time zero. No timing is reported for a fall or unsettled trial. Adjacent cells with different outcomes bracket a change only at the sampled resolution. The transition need not be monotonic. A coloured cell describes its tested point, not all intervening forces and tilts.

The integration audit halves only the physics integration step. It keeps the controller rate, push timing, and fall-detection interval unchanged. It checks a deterministic selection of cells adjacent to different outcomes, not every boundary point.

Replays use the current local configuration. The JSON file preserves this study's configuration and source hashes; rerun the study after changing the model or gains.

Next: measurements and payloads

Step 3 — sensing: add controlled noise and measurement delay with repeatable seeds; compare direct measurements with a state estimator. Use fixed conditions near and away from these boundaries, and report fall frequency over repeated trials. Do not combine noise and delay initially.

Step 4 — payload: add a stationary payload at a defined position, recompute body mass, centre of mass, and inertia consistently, and test the existing gains first. Compare with redesigned gains only as a separate experiment. Begin with static payloads before abrupt load changes, then combine with the sensing study.

Both are planned extensions. Neither is included in the current maps.
