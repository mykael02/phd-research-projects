What this view represents

Live mode computes axle displacement and body pitch using the same 200 Hz physics and controllers as the 2D lab. Recorded mode reads the saved study samples. Each robot occupies a separate display lane; these lanes do not model lateral motion. The wheel radius is 0.045 m and the unloaded body COM is 0.18 m above the axle along the body. With a payload, the yellow marker moves to the combined COM. The shell, wheel width and track width are illustrative, not a CAD design.

The arrow shows the signed horizontal force at the centre of mass during the 0.2 s pulse. Its length is illustrative. Wheel rotation follows the no-slip assumption. A fallen robot stops at its final state; ground impact is not simulated. Live runs last up to 20 simulated seconds. Pausing preserves any remaining force pulse. Recorded mode allows timeline scrubbing; live mode advances only through the physics.

Recovery and unsettled labels describe the full trial, not an instantaneous stability guarantee. No yaw, lateral balance, wheel slip or hardware validation is modelled. Live sensing adds independent Gaussian angle and gyro noise and packet delay. It is a sensing surrogate; accelerometer forces, bias drift and encoder quantisation are not modelled.
